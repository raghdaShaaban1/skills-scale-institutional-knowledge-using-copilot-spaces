# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

This directory centralizes the core project management guidance for OctoAcme. It provides a single place to orient new teammates, find process references, and understand how project work flows from idea to delivery and improvement.

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative project model with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and data-informed decisions. The lifecycle starts with initiation and stakeholder alignment, moves into planning and execution, and continues through release, support, and retrospective learning. The goal is to keep work visible, risks managed, and quality built into delivery rather than bolted on at the end.

## Project management processes used by OctoAcme

- Initiation: Confirm the business need, define success metrics, identify stakeholders and champions, document an initial project one-pager, and decide whether the work should proceed into planning.
- Planning: Turn an approved initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, milestones, dependencies, and a clear Definition of Done.
- Execution and tracking: Run work through a project board, keep PRs small and reviewable, hold regular standups and delivery syncs, and monitor progress against milestones and quality gates.
- Risk management and communication: Track risks, dependencies, and escalations in a risk register, provide regular stakeholder updates, and escalate blockers through the defined PM, Product Lead, and Sponsor path when needed.
- Release and deployment: Verify acceptance criteria, CI checks, and security scans; prepare release notes and rollback plans; deploy through staging and production; and validate outcomes after release.
- Retrospective and continuous improvement: Capture lessons learned after each sprint or milestone, assign action items with owners and due dates, and review improvements in later planning and weekly syncs.
- Roles and personas: Clarify responsibilities across Product Managers, Project Managers, Developers, QA/Testing, and stakeholders so delivery remains coordinated and accountable.
